Naha feels different when you know you’re about to leave. Louder. Closer. Harder to hold at arm’s length.

The city hums constantly, traffic, voices, engines, the low mechanical breath of the nearby army base. Jets cut the sky without warning, shaking windows, rattling thoughts. The sound never quite fades, it just shifts position, like something pacing in the background.

Everything is tinted in soft contradiction. Pastel green cars parked along narrow streets. Houses painted in faded blues and mint tones, sun-worn. And then the neon, sharp and restless, slicing through the night, convenience stores glowing like islands of artificial daylight. The immensity of the Don Quijote stores takes over the night, their endless aisles and looping music bleeding into the streets.

I walk without direction, overwhelmed by it all. The density. The lights. The noise. The feeling that the city is both holding me and pushing me away. Naha doesn’t ask you to stay still. It keeps moving, insists on it, even when you don’t have the strength to follow.

Leaving Okinawa from Naha feels like being gently but firmly pulled back into the world. And as much as the city overwhelms me, it’s that excess, that noise, that uneasy mix of softness and force, that makes saying goodbye hurt more than I expected..
